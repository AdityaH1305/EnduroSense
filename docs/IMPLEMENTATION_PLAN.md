# EnduroSense — Phased Implementation Plan

## Context

EnduroSense extends the intern brief "ML-Based UAV Battery / Remaining Flight-Time Prediction". Instead of only predicting minutes left, it predicts **energy available** (Model A) and **energy required for a specific mission** (Model B). Each prediction comes with calibrated uncertainty, and the two are combined into **P(success)**, which a fleet scheduler uses (approve if P ≥ τ). ## Guiding rules (apply to every phase)

1. **No leakage.** The split unit is the **battery chain**; a single-flight chain is its own group. Every reading from a chain stays on one side of the split. Features are **causal**, meaning they use only data up to time t. Model B uses only inputs **available at planning time**: commanded speed, payload, altitude, forecast wind and distance. Measured airspeed and acceleration are not allowed as Model B inputs.
2. **The test set is locked in Phase 1** and not used again until Phase 7. Phases 3–6 use grouped cross-validation (CV) only, with out-of-fold (OOF) predictions.
3. **Config-driven.** Every threshold lives in `config.yaml`: reserve, τ, chain-matching tolerance, window sizes, seeds, split fractions and quantile levels.
4. **Reproducible.** Fixed seeds; the raw data is never modified; every figure and table is regenerated by numbered scripts; results CSVs record the git commit hash.
5. **Documented.** Every module gets a docstring explaining the idea in plain words, and every phase ends with a short note in `docs/phase_notes/`.
6. **Headline numbers come from real measured data only.** Anything simulated is labelled and kept separate.

## Phase 1: Data preparation, validation and locked split (Weeks 1–2)

> **Status: complete (2026-10-01).** The outcome and the decisions that differ from the text below are in `docs/data_report.md` and `docs/phase_notes/phase1_data.md`: vertical speed is positive going up, motors-off current counts as ground, rest voltages are motors-off, chains use an asymmetric link window, flights are ordered by real timestamps with 4 start times corrected, 71 multi-flight and 60 near-reserve chains, and R6 is forced into test while R5 stays in development. The split is **v2** (`data/splits/split_v2.json`); v1 was invalidated by the ordering fix before any modelling.

**Plain idea:** turn raw sensor rows into a trusted table where every reading knows its flight, phase, energy and battery chain. Then set the test data aside for good.

1. **Load and clean** (`load.py`, `clean.py`):
   - Use explicit dtypes and parse the mixed altitude column. For `"25-50-100-25"`, keep a label, add `alt_cruise_max`, and set `varying_altitude=True`.
   - Check that time increases within each flight and look at the gaps (distribution of Δt, flag gaps over 1 s).
   - Flag voltage and current readings outside physical bounds, and remove spikes with a rolling median/MAD filter.
   - Confirm the current's sign convention.
   - Cache the result to `data/interim/flights.parquet` and write a cleaning report of counts.
2. **Flight-phase segmentation** (`phases.py`):
   - Inputs: smoothed height above take-off, vertical velocity (positive going up in this dataset, despite the README describing north-east-down) and horizontal ground speed.
   - Rules: ground (below 1.5 m and still), climb, cruise (ground speed above 1.5 m/s), descent, and hover/transition. A 1 s majority filter smooths the labels.
   - Validation: plot 12 random flights, and automatically check that every R-route flight follows ground → climb → cruise → descent → ground. Flag any flight that doesn't.
3. **Energy and distance** (`energy.py`): per-sample Wh = V·I·Δt (integrated across the 10 short recording gaps, not capped, so no energy is lost), cumulative energy per flight and per chain, and haversine distance over airborne cruise samples.
4. **Wind** (`wind.py`): an ambient estimate per flight from stationary airborne samples (existing method), plus its standard deviation as a quality flag.
5. **Battery chains** (`chains.py`):
   - Rest voltage for a flight = the next flight's start voltage where available. This fixes the "voltage hasn't recovered after landing" issue.
   - A diagnostic plot per chain (`results/figures/chains/`).
   - Sensitivity table for matching tolerances of 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 V.
   - Investigate chain 19 (flight 81).
   - Manual accept/reject decisions go in `config/chain_overrides.yaml` and are recorded in `docs/chain_review.md`.
6. **Locked split** (`split.py`):
   - About 20% of chain groups go to `test`, stratified so that about 20% of the near-reserve chains land in test (11 of 60 in split v2) and speed and payload are both covered.
   - The rest go to 5 grouped CV folds.
   - Save `data/splits/split_v2.json` with a content hash. Code refuses to load test rows unless a `--final` flag is given.
   - Chains containing R6 (the longest route, ≈820 m) are forced into test; R5 (≈505 m) stays in development for the distance check during development.

**Outputs:** `data/processed/samples.parquet` (per reading), `flights.parquet`, `chains.parquet`, and `docs/data_report.md` with figures.
**Tests:** constant V and I give a known Wh; a synthetic flight gets the correct phases; chains are rebuilt correctly on a toy table; no group appears on both sides of the split.
**Guide checkpoint 1:** data report, plus confirmation of the reserve level.

## Phase 2: Labels and features (Weeks 2–3)

> **Status: complete (2026-10-02).** See `docs/phase_notes/phase2_features.md`. Changes from the text below: labels follow a fitted discharge curve (not a straight line or one pooled slope); pre-flight rest voltage is a running value and phase labels aren't Model A features (strict causality); Model B is built **per leg** (587 legs), with leg time = distance ÷ speed + a learned per-leg overhead; repositioning legs are flagged. Phase 3 must use a *fair* energy-counting baseline that knows the starting charge from the rest voltage.

**Model A: energy available** (`features/model_a.py`)
- **Label:** for chains whose rest voltage reaches 22.6 V, find the point where the reserve is crossed by interpolating in energy between the rest-voltage points on either side. That gives `E_to_reserve` for the chain.
  - Chains that end within 0.3 V above the reserve are extrapolated using the pooled voltage-to-energy slope from the training chains.
  - Other chains get no label and are used only as unlabelled context.
  - Label at time t: `remaining_wh(t) = E_to_reserve − E_used_since_chain_start(t)`.
- **Sampling:** downsample to 1 Hz to reduce near-duplicate rows (≈15–20k samples).
- **Tabular features (all causal):** V, I and P; 10 s and 30 s rolling mean, standard deviation and slope of V and I; voltage sag (flight rest voltage − V); internal resistance (robust regression of ΔV on ΔI over the last 30 s, clipped); energy used since chain start and this flight; time since chain start; flight index within the chain; rest voltage at flight start; phase one-hot.
- **Sequence tensors** for LSTM/GRU: the last 60 s at 2 Hz (120 steps) × [V, I, P, cumulative energy, phase one-hot], normalised with statistics from training folds only.
- **The brief's output:** `minutes_left = remaining_wh / rolling_30s_power × 60`, reported alongside.

**Model B: energy required** (`features/model_b.py`)
- **Targets per flight:** climb energy (Wh), mean cruise power (W), descent energy (Wh), and the ground/transition allowance.
- **Planning-time features only:** commanded speed, payload, cruise altitude and ambient wind speed.
- **`mission.py`:** `MissionSpec(distance_m, speed, payload_out, payload_return, altitude, wind)`.
  - Energy = E_climb + P_cruise·(d/v) + E_descent + allowance. A delivery mission adds a return leg with its own payload.
  - The same function is used for predictions and for rebuilding held-out flights.

**Test:** `test_features_causal.py` changes data after time t and asserts that the features at time t are unchanged.

## Phase 3: Model A comparison (Weeks 3–4)

- **Common interface** (`models/base.py`): `fit`, `predict` and `predict_quantiles`. Tabular models are Ridge (the linear regression, standardised), RandomForest and XGBoost; sequence models are LSTM and GRU in PyTorch on the GPU.
- **Baselines ML must beat** (`baselines.py`):
  - Voltage lookup: isotonic fit of rest voltage → E_to_reserve, minus energy used in the current flight.
  - Energy counting: mean usable capacity from training chains minus energy used.
- **Tuning** uses grouped 5-fold CV inside the training set:
  - RF and XGB: 30-trial random search.
  - LSTM/GRU: a small sweep (hidden size 32/64, 1–2 layers, dropout 0.1–0.3), AdamW with lr 1e-3, early stopping on a validation fold inside the training folds, at most 100 epochs.
- **Metrics:** MAE, RMSE and R² in Wh (OOF across folds, mean ± std), plus the minutes-left MAE. Latency is the median of 1,000 single-sample CPU predictions; model size is measured on disk.
- **Error analysis:** error against true remaining energy, against flight phase, and per chain.

**Outputs:** `results/model_a/cv_metrics.csv`, figures, `models/model_a/`, and `docs/phase_notes/model_a.md`. The best model is chosen by CV MAE, with latency as the tie-break.

## Phase 4: Model B comparison (Weeks 4–5)

- **Physics baseline:** a rotor momentum-theory power model (induced + profile + parasitic), with 3–4 coefficients and the base mass fitted by `scipy.optimize.least_squares`.
- **Models:** Ridge, RF and XGB for each phase target, plus a **physics-hybrid** (XGB trained on the physics model's residual).
  - **LSTM and GRU are not used for Model B**, because its inputs are fixed mission settings rather than a time series. The report states this reasoning; the brief's five-algorithm comparison is carried out on Model A.
- **Mission-level validation:** rebuild each held-out flight's total energy from its settings and report MAE and MAPE.
- **Generalisation tests:** leave out one speed, one payload, one altitude, or one flying day. **Distance test (during development):** train with the R5 flights excluded, then predict them. R6 is only predicted at the final evaluation in Phase 7.

**Outputs:** `results/model_b/*`, `models/model_b/` and `docs/phase_notes/model_b.md`.
**Guide checkpoint 2:** comparison tables for both models.

## Phase 5: Uncertainty and calibration (Weeks 5–6)

- **Predictive distributions** as 19 quantiles (0.05–0.95):
  - XGB: `reg:quantileerror` with multiple quantile levels.
  - Ridge: `QuantileRegressor`.
  - RF: quantiles across the individual trees' predictions.
  - LSTM/GRU: a 5-seed deep ensemble with Gaussian NLL heads, converted to quantiles.
- **Conformal calibration per quantile level:** using OOF calibration groups, shift each quantile so the observed rate of y ≤ Q_q matches q. This gives a calibrated full distribution, not just a single interval.
  - Calibration is done at group level, because readings within a chain are correlated.
- **Model B mission uncertainty:** check whether the component residuals (climb, cruise and descent) are correlated, using OOF data.
  - If they are correlated, sample the **joint residual tuples per flight** (empirical bootstrap) instead of assuming independence.
- **Metrics:** coverage at 50, 80, 90 and 95%, mean interval width, average pinball loss, and reliability diagrams.
- **Target:** CV coverage of the 90% interval between 88% and 92%.

## Phase 6: Feasibility, what-if evaluation and fleet simulation (Weeks 6–7)

- **`feasibility.py`:** `p_success(qA, qB)`, computed by inverse-CDF sampling (10k draws) of the margin M = A − B. There's also a Gaussian closed form for speed. The decision is GO if p ≥ τ.
- **Correlation between the models:** check the correlation of Model A and Model B errors on OOF pairs. If it's clearly non-zero, sample from the joint residuals.
- **`whatif.py`:**
  - Battery states are real snapshots, one every 10 s, from OOF chains, with the true remaining energy known.
  - Missions are real flights, plus compound missions made of 2–4 flights, with the true energy known.
  - **Truth:** the mission succeeds if true remaining ≥ true mission energy.
  - Generate about 20k pairs, reported both at the natural mix and at a stratified mix with at least 30% borderline cases (|margin| < 5 Wh).
- **Policies:**
  - P1: minutes left ≥ mission duration (the brief's approach).
  - P2: energy point estimates.
  - P3: EnduroSense, P ≥ τ.
  - P4: an oracle with the true values.
  - P1 and P2 are also swept across **safety margins**, so each policy produces a curve of unsafe approvals against wasted refusals and the comparison is fair.
- **`scheduler.py` (fleet simulation):** 4 drones and a task queue drawn from real flight settings.
  - Each drone's state is a **real snapshot in a real chain**. After a mission, the drone moves to the snapshot in the same chain where the energy used has risen by the mission's true energy. A recharge jumps to a fresh chain start. Everything stays grounded in measured telemetry.
  - Metrics: tasks completed, unsafe assignments, recharges and battery left at recharge, averaged over 200 seeded simulated days (mean ± 95% CI).
- τ is chosen using OOF data only.

## Phase 7: Final locked-test evaluation and reproducibility (Week 7)

- `scripts/10_final_test.py --final`: opens the test split **once** and runs the final Model A, Model B, calibration, what-if and fleet evaluation on the test groups.
- `scripts/run_all.py`: rebuilds everything from the raw CSV to the final tables.
- Final results go to `results/final/`, with a git-hash-stamped summary in `docs/results_summary.md`.
- Check the result against the proposed success criteria:
  - Model A beats both baselines.
  - Model B's MAPE is about 5% or less, including on R5 (development) and R6 (final test).
  - 90% interval coverage is between 88% and 92%.
  - The P3 curve is better than P1 and P2.
- **Guide checkpoint 3:** full results.

## Phase 8: Streamlit demo, report and slides (Weeks 7–8, plus a buffer week)

- `app/streamlit_app.py` loads the saved models only and caches them; nothing trains inside the app.
  - **Mission check page:** pick a real battery state (test chain plus a time slider) or a preset; fill in a mission form (distance, speed, payload out and back, altitude, wind). Show both energy distributions overlaid, P(success), a GO/NO-GO badge and what minutes-left would have said.
  - **Fleet page:** run the simulation with a chosen policy and τ, with P1–P4 metrics side by side.
  - **Models page:** comparison tables, latency and calibration plots.
- **"Judge moment" preset:** three missions (short and calm, long into a headwind, heavy payload) where P1 approves all three and EnduroSense flags the risky one. These are chosen from real test-set what-if cases, not made up.
- Final report (docx) and slides built from `results/final/`.
- **Stretch goals, if time allows:** physics-hybrid refinements, a clearly labelled simulated high-wind stress test, and latency measured on a Raspberry Pi.

## Fallbacks decided in advance

- **Fewer than 15 usable labelled chains after review:** add labels from the pooled training voltage-to-energy curve for the other chains, flag them as "semi-measured", and report results with and without them.
- **LSTM/GRU don't beat the tabular models:** report it as a finding, and use the best tabular model in the demo.
- **Conformal coverage misses the target** because of dependence within chains: aggregate the calibration scores per chain and widen the intervals.
- **Behind schedule by week 6:** cut the stretch goals and simplify the fleet page. The what-if evaluation stays, because it's the core result.
